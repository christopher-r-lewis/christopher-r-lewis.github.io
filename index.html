About

Support experience with a systems mindset

I moved into technical work from the support floor. Years of answering customer problems directly showed me where our tools and processes broke down, and that turned into owning the platforms behind them.

I enjoy understanding how systems work, making technical information easier to follow, and building tools that address everyday operational problems. Most of my work sits between the people reporting an issue and the teams who fix it — clarifying what is actually happening, documenting it, and making the handoff repeatable.

When a problem is well enough understood to build against, I write down what the tool needs to collect, who reviews it, and what has to happen at each step. From there I use AI tools such as Cursor to produce a working version, then review what comes back against those requirements and decide what to correct or build next. The tools speed up the drafting; the decisions about what is needed and whether the result actually fits the workflow stay with me.

Problem

Customer Care escalation requests need consistent information and a clear review process before reaching engineering.

My contribution

I defined the intake requirements, the role-based review workflows, and the criteria an AI-assisted review would need to apply. The prototype now has request-specific forms and review views for different responsibilities, and I’m working through improvements before testing with the intended users.

How it gets built

I use Cursor to build and refine the prototype, reviewing what it produces against the requirements I set and identifying improvements as the project develops. Cursor is how the prototype gets written; the review criteria are part of the design for how requests should be assessed, and that work is still ahead of where the prototype stands today.
